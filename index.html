Sedation of the Severely Agitated Patient

A Flowchart for Emergency Medicine

Severely agitated patient requiring emergency sedation

1. Call for help

  • Security
  • Inform most senior clinician on shop floor + NIC
  • Call Police at discretion of Security / Senior Clinician

2. Preparation & Team Brief

  • Ensure capacity assessment is valid
  • Attempt verbal de-escalation
  • Ensure correct / sufficient / trained staff & assign roles
  • Place 'Emergency Sedation' notice outside bay
  • Proceed only if immediate threat exists

3. Throughout Procedure

  • Maintain personal safety
  • Apply full monitoring ASAP
  • Provide early oxygenation
  • Ensure airway equipment is ready
  • Monitor for chest compression
  • Have rescue plan (A,B,C,D)
Apply Physical Restraint & Obtain Access
Administer Sedation If IV/IO Unavailable / Unsafe use IM

Ketamine 4mg/kg (IM)

Ketamine 0.5-1mg/kg (IV/IO)

Second-Line:

Lorazepam/Haloperidol at clinician's discretion

Wait for Peak Onset (IM: 3-5m, IV: 30s, IO: 2m)
Assess Sedation Level If inadequate after 2 doses or oversedated, consider ICU input. If complications unmanageable in ED, contact ICU.
Patient Adequately Sedated

Post-Sedation Management

  • Transfer to resus/monitored bay ASAP
  • Regular observations and monitoring
  • Check Blood Gas, FBC, U&E, LFT, troponin, CK, clotting & ECG
  • Consider imaging, blood cultures, toxicology screen, ammonia level
  • Complete all documentation including RADAR
  • If possible, debrief all participants & witnesses

Key Considerations

  • Reversible Causes: Hypoxia, Hypoglycaemia, Head Injury, Sepsis, Intoxication, Post-ictal state.
  • Governance: EPIC should be alerted/consulted. Must be led or supervised by IAC competent clinician.
  • Second-Line Agents: Consider Lorazepam, Haloperidol or other agents at the discretion of an IAC competent clinician if ketamine is contraindicated.
  • Post-Sedation Care: Perform a full secondary survey (ABCDE).
